SPHEREx is a NASA space telescope that photographs the whole sky in infrared light, again and again. When two photos taken months apart are compared, anything that moved stands out: comets, asteroids and even undiscovered planets. This page lets you make that comparison yourself.

  1. Pick a sky regionEach region is a small patch of sky.
  2. Watch it blinkThe picture flips between two dates. Fixed stars stay still and movers jump.
  3. Click a circled objectSee a close-up image, what it is and why it matters.


Demo sky: positions are synthetic, made to show the idea. Real SPHEREx images plug into the same viewer through the upload box. Drag to pan, scroll to zoom, click any object.

How the movement is measured

Step 1: read the longitude and latitude of the object in picture 1. Step 2: read them again in pictures 2, 3 and 4. Step 3: for each pair of pictures, work out the straight-line (Euclidean) distance between the two positions.

AI analyst

Each candidate is scored for how likely it is a real moving object, its path is fitted across the four passes, and a plain-language explanation is written from the measured numbers.

How the scoring works

Hybrid AI: a transparent scoring model plus a small neural network trained live in your browser (see the Train AI page). Inputs: how straight the path is across passes (least-squares line fit), signal strength, and distance from the frame edge. A neural-network filter trained on real SPHEREx data and an LLM explainer are the planned upgrades.

    🧠 Neural network · real object vs fake (trained live)

    Model: MLP 121 → 16 → 8 → 1 (tanh, sigmoid), trained by mini-batch gradient descent with momentum on synthetic 11×11 image stamps. Real = a point-like moving source. Fake = hot pixel, cosmic-ray streak, misalignment dipole, pure noise, smeared blob. Distance itself is still measured with exact Euclidean maths.
